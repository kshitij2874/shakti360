Understanding Menopause: A Guide for Women Over 41

Menopause is a natural biological milestone marking the permanent cessation of menstruation, diagnosed retrospectively after 12 consecutive months without a period. The average age of menopause in Indian women is 46–48 years, slightly earlier than the global average of 51 years.

Perimenopause — The Transition Phase:
Perimenopause typically begins 4–8 years before menopause, often in the early-to-mid 40s. During this phase, estrogen levels fluctuate unpredictably, causing:
- Irregular periods (shorter or longer cycles, heavier or lighter flow).
- Hot flashes and night sweats (vasomotor symptoms), affecting 60–80% of women.
- Sleep disturbances.
- Mood changes, irritability, and anxiety.
- Vaginal dryness and discomfort during intercourse.
- Decreased libido.
- Difficulty concentrating and memory lapses ("brain fog").

When to See a Doctor During Perimenopause:
- Bleeding between periods or after intercourse.
- Periods occurring more frequently than every 21 days.
- Extremely heavy bleeding (soaking a pad/tampon every hour).
- Periods lasting more than 7 days.
- Any bleeding after menopause has been confirmed.

Post-Menopausal Health Risks:
The decline in estrogen after menopause significantly increases the risk of several conditions:

1. Osteoporosis: Women lose up to 20% of bone density in the 5–7 years after menopause. AIIMS guidelines recommend:
   - Calcium intake: 1,200 mg/day (dietary + supplements if needed).
   - Vitamin D: 800–1,000 IU/day.
   - Weight-bearing exercise: 30 minutes, 5 days/week.
   - DEXA scan for bone density assessment, recommended at age 50 or at menopause onset.

2. Cardiovascular Disease: Estrogen has a protective effect on blood vessels. After menopause, women's cardiovascular risk rises to match men's. Key preventive measures:
   - Regular blood pressure monitoring (target: <130/80 mmHg).
   - Lipid profile check annually.
   - Maintain BMI 18.5–24.9.
   - 150 minutes/week of moderate aerobic activity.
   - Heart-healthy diet: reduce salt (<5 g/day), increase omega-3 fatty acids.

3. Urogenital Atrophy: Thinning of vaginal and urethral tissues can cause recurrent UTIs, vaginal dryness, and urinary incontinence. Local estrogen therapy (vaginal cream/pessary) is considered safe and effective.

4. Mental Health: Perimenopausal and post-menopausal women have a 2–4x increased risk of depression. Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) has shown effectiveness for managing menopausal mood symptoms.

Management Options:
Non-Hormonal Approaches:
- Lifestyle modifications: regular exercise, stress management (yoga, meditation), balanced diet.
- Layered clothing and cool environments for hot flash management.
- Vaginal moisturisers and lubricants.
- Phytoestrogens (soy, flaxseed) — evidence is modest but some women report benefit.
- CBT for mood and sleep symptoms.

Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT):
HRT remains the most effective treatment for vasomotor symptoms. Current AIIMS and IMS (Indian Menopause Society) guidelines state:
- HRT is appropriate for women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause onset.
- Lowest effective dose for the shortest duration needed.
- Combined estrogen + progesterone for women with an intact uterus.
- Estrogen alone for women post-hysterectomy.
- Regular review every 12 months.
- Contraindicated in women with a history of breast cancer, active liver disease, unexplained vaginal bleeding, or history of blood clots.

Indian Government Schemes for Older Women's Health:
- Ayushman Bharat (PMJAY): Covers hospitalisation up to ₹5 lakh/year for eligible families, including orthopaedic and cardiac procedures relevant to post-menopausal complications.
- National Programme for Prevention and Control of Cancer, Diabetes, CVD and Stroke (NPCDCS): Free screening at Health and Wellness Centres.
- Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana: Provides physical aids (walking sticks, spectacles, hearing aids) to senior citizens from BPL families.

Screening Recommendations After 40:
- Annual: blood pressure, fasting blood sugar, lipid profile, thyroid function.
- Every 2 years: mammography (breast cancer screening).
- Every 3 years: Pap smear / cervical screening (until age 65).
- At menopause: DEXA scan for osteoporosis.
- As needed: colonoscopy (after age 50), eye examination.

[Source: AIIMS Menopause Clinic Guidelines 2023, Indian Menopause Society Position Statement 2024, WHO Menopause and Midlife Health Report, ICMR Guidelines on Osteoporosis Management]
