Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY) — Safe Motherhood for Indian Women (Ages 25–40)

Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY) is a centrally sponsored scheme under the National Health Mission (NHM), launched on 12 April 2005, to reduce maternal and neonatal mortality by promoting institutional deliveries among pregnant women, especially from Below Poverty Line (BPL) families.

Objective:
The primary goal of JSY is to reduce maternal mortality ratio (MMR) and infant mortality rate (IMR) by incentivising institutional deliveries and providing antenatal and postnatal care support.

Eligibility:
- All pregnant women delivering in government health centres are eligible in Low Performing States (LPS): Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Bihar, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Rajasthan, Odisha, Assam, and Jammu & Kashmir.
- In High Performing States (HPS): only BPL women aged 19 and above, for up to 2 live births.
- SC and ST women are eligible regardless of age or number of children.

Cash Assistance Under JSY:
Low Performing States:
- Rural: ₹1,400 to the mother + ₹600 to the ASHA worker = ₹2,000 total.
- Urban: ₹1,000 to the mother + ₹400 to the ASHA worker = ₹1,400 total.

High Performing States:
- Rural: ₹700 to the mother + ₹600 to the ASHA worker.
- Urban: ₹600 to the mother + ₹400 to the ASHA worker.

Role of ASHA Workers:
Accredited Social Health Activists (ASHAs) are the backbone of JSY at the community level. Their responsibilities include:
- Identifying pregnant women and facilitating registration for antenatal care (ANC).
- Ensuring at least 3 ANC check-ups including TT immunisation and IFA tablets.
- Arranging transport to the nearest health facility for delivery.
- Facilitating post-natal visits within 7 days of delivery.
- Promoting exclusive breastfeeding and family planning counselling.

Antenatal Care (ANC) Essentials:
The Government of India recommends a minimum of 4 ANC visits during pregnancy:
1. First trimester (within 12 weeks): Registration, blood tests (Hb, blood group, HIV, VDRL), urine tests, weight, blood pressure.
2. 14–26 weeks: TT-1 vaccination, iron-folic acid supplementation, anomaly scan.
3. 28–34 weeks: TT-2, glucose screening, growth monitoring.
4. 36 weeks onwards: Birth preparedness, delivery plan, complication readiness.

Warning Signs During Pregnancy (Seek Immediate Help):
- Severe headache or blurred vision (may indicate pre-eclampsia).
- Vaginal bleeding at any stage.
- High fever with or without chills.
- Severe abdominal pain.
- Reduced or absent fetal movement after 28 weeks.
- Swelling of face and hands (beyond normal pregnancy swelling).
- Convulsions.

Call 108 (National Ambulance Service) or 102 (Janani Express) for emergency pregnancy transport.

Related Government Schemes:
- Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana (PMMVY): ₹5,000 cash incentive for first live birth in 3 instalments for women aged 19+.
- Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA): Free ANC check-ups on the 9th of every month at government facilities.
- LaQshya: Initiative to improve quality of care in labour rooms and maternity operation theatres.
- PMJAY (Ayushman Bharat): Covers up to ₹5 lakh per family per year for secondary and tertiary hospitalisation, including high-risk pregnancies and C-sections.

Nutritional Guidance During Pregnancy:
ICMR recommends an additional 350 kcal/day during pregnancy. Key nutrients:
- Iron: 35 mg/day (doubled from non-pregnant requirement).
- Folic acid: 500 µg/day (critical in first trimester to prevent neural tube defects).
- Calcium: 1,200 mg/day.
- Protein: additional 23 g/day.

[Source: Ministry of Health and Family Welfare — JSY Guidelines 2023, National Health Mission Portal, ICMR Dietary Guidelines 2020, WHO Recommendations on Antenatal Care 2024]
