Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) — Securing Your Daughter's Future

Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) is a Government of India savings scheme launched on 22 January 2015 under the "Beti Bachao, Beti Padhao" campaign. It is designed to encourage parents to build a fund for the education and marriage expenses of their girl child.

Key Features:
- Account can be opened for a girl child below 10 years of age.
- Maximum 2 accounts per family (one per girl child; exception for twins/triplets).
- Can be opened at any Post Office or authorised commercial bank (SBI, PNB, BOB, ICICI, HDFC, Axis, etc.).
- Account is in the name of the girl child, operated by the guardian until she turns 18.

Interest Rate:
- Current rate (Q1 FY2026): 8.2% per annum, compounded annually.
- Reviewed and notified quarterly by the Ministry of Finance.
- Among the highest interest rates offered by any government small savings scheme.

Investment Limits:
- Minimum deposit: ₹250 per financial year.
- Maximum deposit: ₹1,50,000 per financial year.
- Deposits can be made in lump sum or in multiple instalments.
- If minimum deposit is not made in a year, a penalty of ₹50 is charged, and the account becomes inactive (can be reactivated).

Tenure and Maturity:
- Deposit period: 15 years from the date of opening.
- Maturity: 21 years from the date of opening, or on the marriage of the girl after age 18 (whichever is earlier).
- After 15 years, no further deposits are required, but the balance continues to earn interest until maturity.

Tax Benefits (Section 80C — EEE Status):
- Deposits qualify for tax deduction under Section 80C (up to ₹1,50,000/year).
- Interest earned is fully tax-free.
- Maturity amount is fully tax-free.
- SSY enjoys EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) status — the most favourable tax treatment.

Partial Withdrawal:
- Allowed after the girl turns 18 years old.
- Up to 50% of the balance at the end of the preceding financial year can be withdrawn.
- Purpose: higher education expenses (admission fee, hostel charges).
- Required documents: admission proof from a recognised institution.

Premature Closure:
- Allowed only in specific circumstances:
  a) Death of the account holder (girl child) — balance paid to guardian.
  b) Life-threatening illness of the account holder.
  c) Change in residency status (NRI status — account must be closed).
  d) Marriage of the girl child after age 18.
- In case of premature closure due to hardship, interest is paid at the applicable SSY rate (not reduced to savings rate).

Illustrative Returns:
If a parent deposits ₹1,50,000 per year for 15 years at 8.2%:
- Total deposits: ₹22,50,000
- Estimated maturity value (at 21 years): ~₹71,82,000
- Interest earned: ~₹49,32,000

If a parent deposits ₹12,500 per month (₹1,50,000/year) for 15 years:
- Same total deposits and estimated returns as above.

Even small deposits grow significantly:
- ₹250/year for 15 years → approximately ₹10,870 at maturity.
- ₹1,000/month for 15 years → approximately ₹5,74,000 at maturity.

How to Open an SSY Account:
1. Visit nearest Post Office or bank branch.
2. Fill Form-1 (SSY Account Opening Form).
3. Documents required:
   - Birth certificate of the girl child.
   - Identity proof of guardian (Aadhaar, PAN).
   - Address proof of guardian.
   - 2 passport-size photos of guardian.
4. Make the initial deposit (minimum ₹250).
5. Receive passbook with account details.

Comparison with Other Options:
- SSY (8.2%) vs PPF (7.1%): SSY offers ~1.1% higher returns.
- SSY vs FD (6–7%): SSY offers higher returns + tax-free interest.
- SSY vs mutual funds: Mutual funds may offer higher returns but carry market risk. SSY is risk-free with guaranteed returns.

[Source: Ministry of Finance — Sukanya Samriddhi Account Scheme Notification 2019, India Post Official Guidelines, Department of Economic Affairs Quarterly Rate Notifications FY2025-26]
